RULE-BASED AI AND SHARED GAME RULES
=================================

AI means artificial intelligence. UI means user interface.
LLM means large language model.
The rule-based layer always runs, including when no LLM is enabled.
It generates legal actions and scores them.
Without the model, the AI uses library response text and does not hold model-based summits.

The model selects from legal actions and writes messages.
It cannot create an action that the rules forbid.
See documentation/llm_ai.txt for model settings, decisions, prompts, and request control.

This guide covers valuation, research, combat, diplomacy, and shared player-mode rules.
See documentation/ai_movement.txt for unit orders, movement caches, aircraft AI, and movement tuning.
The combat and display rules also apply to human players where stated.
See documentation/diplo_stuff.txt for diplomacy timing and effects.
See documentation/air_stuff.txt for the full aircraft rules.

AI MODULES
----------

map_logic/ai/ contains these shared components:

ai_world.py
    One map snapshot per turn for strength, border, and relation queries.
ai_unit_eval.py
    Unit value from its stats and the country's resource scarcity.
    Do not maintain a separate list of preferred units.
ai_tech_eval.py
    Technology value from the things it unlocks.
ai_personality.py
    Five scenario-seeded traits. Saved personalities are reproducible.
ai_opinion.py
    War, peace, trade, and alliance preference scores.
ai_candidates.py
    Legal action generation and ranking.
ai_commitments.py
    Promises and the cost of breaking them.
ai_construction.py, ai_research.py, ai_diplomacy.py
    Production, research, and proactive diplomacy.
ai_movement.py
    Unit orders. See documentation/ai_movement.txt.

RESEARCH INDEXES
----------------

data/queries.py indexes unit research requirements and family tiers.
Production and AI use the same unlock checks.
New research levels reuse the index without parsing the unit library again.
Invalidate it after library-name edits, research-year changes, gate changes, obsolescence changes, or JSON refreshes.
This includes changes made in place.

These indexes are temporary. They are not saved game fields.

AIRCRAFT
--------

Aerospace is a separate research and production category.
air_role and air_range_px in unit_data.json define flight.
production_group and land movement speed do not define flight.
Numbered aircraft levels use the shared research, production, and upgrade rules.
Read current research settings in data/json/research_template.json.
Read current stats, costs, and capabilities in data/json/unit_data.json.
data/generators/recipes.py defines data generation.

Shared queries read aircraft capabilities from the current unit library.
queries.air_order_radius calculates reach for each order type.
air_consumable and air_interception_immune control strike consumption and interception eligibility.

The mission selector offers No mission, Move, Strike, Transport, and Defend area.
Defend area uses weakest, strongest, or random priority.
All choices stay visible. Unavailable missions and flight from an invalid base are disabled.
Move and Strike start target selection while preserving the current order.
Use a left-click or the configured move-order click to select a valid target.
Escape cancels target selection without changing the current order.

Use a move-order click on enemy territory or visible enemy units to strike.
This includes hostile units on friendly territory.
A click on accessible land without hostile units issues relocation.
Aircraft cannot land in enemy or unclaimed territory.
No mission or the order cancel control cancels the current order.
The mission button always opens the selector.
Active Move and Strike orders accept new destinations without cancellation and preserve their mission type.
This also applies to aircraft with a ground route.
Change or cancel an active defense mission before using a move-order click.
Active defense and strikes show only yellow mission range. Active relocation shows only green movement range.
Idle aircraft show green relocation range until the pointer targets enemy territory or visible hostile units.
That target switches the preview to yellow strike range. One-use weapons always show yellow strike range.
Outside all provinces or over UI controls, the preview keeps its previous range and color.

Mission icons show the current choice.
Air Move shows relocation, including a one-use weapon's ground path.
No Mission shows an idle aircraft.
The upgrade button uses the shared numbered-family upgrade helper.
Named aircraft designs remain separate production unlocks.
air_transport_capacity defines cargo capability and capacity.
Transport aircraft use only Move and Transport missions.
Their purchase and research value uses capacity and range. They have a separate transport role.
Cargo stays outside map stacks and combat. Upkeep and army membership include cargo.
See documentation/air_stuff.txt for cargo controls, damage, saves, and multiplayer commands.

Set Mission applies a choice to eligible aircraft in the selected group.
The button opens the selector even when selected aircraft have active missions.
It shows all choices and disables choices unavailable to every eligible aircraft.
It rechecks aircraft identity, permissions, and capabilities when applying a choice or target.
Group strikes use the same move-order click as single aircraft.
A movement command preserves all selected orders if any unit cannot reach its destination.
Group Move and Strike target selection also preserves all captured orders if any captured unit becomes invalid.
Unsupported group defense choices preserve those aircraft's orders.
No mission clears eligible aircraft orders.
Both multiplayer systems use their existing per-unit commands and validate each order.

After interception, strikes use one simultaneous combat exchange against the garrison.
All attacking aircraft participate. Garrison units retain terrain width, front ranks, and reserves.
Interception also has no aircraft width limit.

Each coalition's engaged aircraft share the attack multiplier from queries.air_stack_attack_efficiency.
The multiplier decreases with stack size and has a minimum.
See documentation/air_stuff.txt for the formula and counting rules.
Defenders can damage or destroy aircraft.

queries.unit_target_damage_multiplier supplies shared immunity and damage modifiers.
Aircraft air-to-air bonuses apply only during interception.
Survivors keep their losses at the launch base.
Flight never captures territory.

air_damages_forts defines fort damage capability in unit data.
Research details show it.
Player and AI orders use the same turn processor.
See documentation/ai_movement.txt for aircraft AI decisions.

Aircraft can load into Convoys at a coast and unload on land.
Transport preserves their health fraction and original stats.
Hostile ground combat destroys aircraft before lane assignment or damage exchange.
Neutral aircraft and aircraft transported at sea are not destroyed by that ground-combat rule.
UI and AI estimates use the same casualty rule.

On load, old aircraft Trucks unpack on land or become Convoys at sea.
Old Truck conversion orders become Convoy orders on a coast and cancel inland.
Production values aerospace as the AIR role.
Production and research use the same aircraft valuation.
Ground strikes receive no air-to-air bonus.
Interception value depends on visible enemy aircraft and available air cover.
Mission estimates use stack efficiency and the shared battle rules.
Owned and queued reusable aircraft reduce the need for more interception strength.
Interception-immune enemies do not create interception demand.

Unit names do not determine purchase preferences. There is no fixed fighter-to-bomber ratio.

air_pressure = hostile_air_attack / (hostile_ground_attack + hostile_air_attack + friendly_interception_attack).
Friendly interception attack includes each aircraft's shared air-to-air multiplier.
A zero denominator gives zero air pressure.
The evaluator applies interception multipliers before it subtracts enemy aircraft defense.
It does not add the separate bombardment valuation to aircraft interception or strike value.

Research uses the existing ahead-of-time penalty, including aerospace.
Recruitment buildings use the General Buildings panel.
Read current building settings in data/json/building_data.json and research settings in data/json/research_template.json.
See documentation/air_stuff.txt for the full aircraft rules.

DIPLOMACY
---------

War desire includes the power of a target's independence guarantors.
Their possible entry into the war reduces the desire to attack.

Proactive claims and declarations consider land neighbors and nearby overseas countries.
AI_NEARBY_SEA_TILES limits the connected navigable water tiles between countries.
Lakes do not count. Overseas claims target the nearby coast.
Without a shared land front, both odds calculations use overall power.
Wargoal, truce, faction, and turn restrictions still apply.

AI hosts accept valid Military Attach? requests unless the sender supports one of their enemies.
Support means active volunteer divisions or an attach? observing that enemy.
An existing conflicting attach? is revoked on the following turn.
An attach? shares the host's battlefield vision.
It withdraws immediately if the host leaves its wars or becomes hostile to the sender.

Volunteer divisions use the same conflict rule.
AI hosts reject offers from countries supporting an enemy.
If hostile hosts accept simultaneous offers, they send the divisions home on the following turn after the conflict becomes known.

VALUATION RULES
---------------

Front-rank defense
    apply_group_damage uses sum(max(0, volley/N - defense)).
    N counts the legal targets in that lane's opposing front rank.
    It does not count reserve units.
    Front-rank units absorb damage through defense.
    Reserves replace losses but do not reduce the current volley.
    AI_RESERVE_DEPTH limits how many ranks the AI values.

Building research
    Value the added output of an upgrade, not the new building's total output.
    The upgrade removes the previous building level.
    A province can queue only the next building in its chain.
    Do not credit an unreachable upgrade as an immediate benefit.

Purchase and disbanding
    Use ai_unit_eval's marginal value for both decisions.
    A separate raw-attack ranking can make the AI buy and disband the same type.

Obsolescence
    Aircraft, land units, and naval units use constants.OBSOLESCENCE_RULES.
    A researched replacement removes its older family from default production and AI build candidates.
    The disband pass uses the same obsolescence query.
    Players can still choose researched older aircraft through Custom production.
    Existing units and production queues remain intact in every play mode.

TUNING
------

Shared tuning values are in data/constants.py.
Read their current values there.
See documentation/ai_movement.txt for movement-specific tuning.

AI_WAR_DESIRE_THRESHOLD
    Minimum war desire for a declaration.
AI_LINE_SPEND_RATIO
    Spending balance between unit numbers and firepower.
AI_W_SOAK
    Value of defense that absorbs damage in the front rank.
    Reserves do not absorb the current volley.
AI_RESERVE_DEPTH
    Number of ranks worth filling.
AI_TECH_ECONOMY_WEIGHT
    Research balance between industry and military needs.
AI_BUILDING_PAYBACK_TURNS
    Time allowed for a building to recover its cost.
    Keep this separate from AI_TECH_ECONOMY_HORIZON, which compares income with military value.
AI_MAX_BUILDING_RESEARCH_LEAD
    Building research levels permitted ahead of existing construction.
AI_COMMITMENT_WEIGHT
    Restraint from commitments before loyalty modifies it.
AI_MAX_ACTIONS_PER_TURN
    Limit on a country's proactive actions.
AI_UPKEEP_TARGETS
    Income share permitted for upkeep of each resource.
    Fuel limits can strongly affect army size.
    UPKEEP_MODIFIERS defines the resource upkeep rates.
COMBAT_WIDTH
    Default combat-width budget for a whole tile.
    This is a source setting, without a scenario or editor field.
COMBAT_WIDTH_BY_TERRAIN
    Terrain overrides for combat width.
    Battle controls, sidebar counts, estimates, and resolution use the same width.
    Head-on crossings use the narrower endpoint's width in either direction.
MIN_LANE_SLOTS_PER_SIDE
    Minimum allowance for a coalition side in one lane.
    share_slots divides that allowance among its countries.
    Each country present also gets at least one unit slot.
    These minimums can exceed the tile's width.
LANE_SLOTS_TYPICAL
    Default per-side slots for general purchase and research estimates.
    Movement, border strength, and enemy-front estimates use the actual terrain width.

AI PERSONALITY
--------------

Use the map editor's AI Personality button to edit and pin traits.
Pinned traits do not regenerate.
Scenario data can also set overrides:

  "ai_personality_overrides": {"German Reich": {"aggression": 0.9}}

The traits are aggression, ambition, caution, loyalty, and trust.
Each uses a value from 0.0 to 1.0.
Without an override, the scenario seed generates reproducible traits.
Generated traits need not match historical behavior.
tests/test_editor_menu_layout.py checks that editor controls do not overlap.

PUPPETS AND COUNTRY REMOVAL
--------------------------

A puppet can declare war or negotiate peace only with its master.
It cannot independently join, receive an invitation to, or leave a faction.
pull_puppets_into_faction moves puppets with their master.

queries.can_negotiate_peace and queries.can_choose_own_faction define the shared restrictions.
queries.can_invite_to_faction permits invitations only from the leader of an existing faction.
treaty_effects.apply_treaty_effect and faction_actions enforce them.
This includes crossing invitations.

A landless faction member remains active as a government in exile.
It keeps its units, diplomacy, faction wars, leadership, and pre-war border record.
Regaining a province ends exile.
If every faction member becomes landless, the faction disbands and ordinary cleanup applies.

Other countries that lose their last province leave factions, wars, and pending offers.
A player-created puppet, or a rebellion defeated while still at war, also loses its record and cores.
A scenario's integrated puppet keeps its cores after annexation.
See documentation/diplo_stuff.txt for the exile and diplomacy rules.

LANE COMBAT
-----------

A combat side is a coalition of countries fighting together against an enemy.
Coalition members must be friendly in both directions and not at war with each other.
A contested tile has one lane for each pair of hostile sides.
Two allied countries fighting two allied enemies share one lane.
Without alliances, each hostile country pair has its own lane.

Divide terrain-adjusted combat width among the lanes.
Apply MIN_LANE_SLOTS_PER_SIDE to each side.
Every hostile country pair on the tile must share a lane.
Pairing only individual countries can split an allied battle incorrectly or omit legal opponents.

combat_rules.share_slots divides a side's slots fairly among its countries.
It repeatedly shares remaining slots among countries that still need them.
Unused slots from a small force become available to larger allied forces.
A country cannot receive less than its even share when it has enough units.
Each country present gets at least one unit slot, even if this exceeds the width.

A country fires only at countries it is personally fighting.
An ally's other wars do not create new targets.
Damage does not cross lane boundaries.
Each country fires once per turn.
A unit assigned to several lanes divides its attack among them.
It can take damage from each lane.

A country assigns a unit to every lane before adding a second unit to any lane.
If units are insufficient, it shares units across lanes instead of abandoning a fight.
Neither a lone defender nor a small attacking force can prevent another hostile side from participating.

combat_stance is a reserve preference.
Honor it only when another unit can fill the required front slot.
A country cannot avoid combat by holding every unit in reserve.
Movement clears both lane orders.

Reserves neither fire nor take ordinary lane damage.
They recover morale while front units fight.
Players can rotate units through the Battle screen.
See documentation/ai_movement.txt for AI rotation, bombardment, and attack checks.

Bombardment is free and receives no return fire.
It is outside the lane system and can hit reserves.
A bombarding unit can still fight on its own tile.

DISBANDING AND FUEL
------------------

Disbanding is a last resort.
Do not disband units in combat, beside an enemy, or of a type currently being produced.
If all candidates are at the front, disband none.
Use the standing army's upkeep deficit. Do not include queued units in that deficit.

Never disband the tactical player's division.
Skip it during candidate selection so the AI can consider another unit.
ai_construction.tactical_player_unit identifies the protected division.
Protection depends on active tactical mode, not just a retained player_unit attribute.

Check fuel when purchasing.
A fuel shortage excludes fuel-consuming units from affordable candidates and saving plans.
It does not block units that need no fuel.

RESPONSE TEXT AND MESSAGE METADATA
----------------------------------

data/json/ai_responses.json stores replies for use without a model.
It uses the shared JSON cache.
A response key contains a string or a list of variant groups.
A group has interchangeable "lines" and can have a "when" condition.
The most specific eligible group wins. Select one of its lines randomly.

Supported "when" keys:
    strength: weaker, even, stronger
    at_war
    same_faction
    opinion: hostile, cold, warm

Use ai_prompts.resolve() to read a response.
AI_FALLBACK_RESPONSES remains a mapping for callers without a comparison country.
Edit the JSON to add lines.

WAR_DECLARATION stores text in the queued entry's prose field.
Its message field holds the wargoal required by queued_message.
Other actions with queued_message must also keep rule data separate from prose.

Messages also retain the action that produced them.
constants.MESSAGE_CATEGORIES maps actions to display categories:
WAR, JOIN WAR, PEACE, TRADE, FACTION, ACCESS, PUPPET, and ALLIANCE.
The protocol type remains TEXT or DIPLOMACY.
That value controls popups and the gold message bubble.

CALL_TO_ARMS and JOIN_WARS use JOIN WAR.
They combine existing wars instead of declaring a new one.
tests/test_spectator_messages.py checks that category labels fit their table column.

See documentation/llm_ai.txt for model voice rules and the llm authorship field.

TACTICAL PLAY
-------------

A tactical player commands one division.
The host country remains AI-controlled.
queries.get_active_ai_nations removes the human-country exclusion while tactical_mode is active.

- The AI cannot disband the player's division.
- Orders and Battle allow edits only to that division.
  They still display other country units.
- Diplomatic popups for the host's inbox do not interrupt tactical play.
  diplomatic_popups.spawn_popups_for_player still marks those messages as shown.
  This prevents old host messages from appearing after the player declares independence.

Declaring independence ends tactical mode.
map_screen.player_unit can remain after that change.
Always check the mode instead of inferring it from the attribute.

SPECTATOR PLAY
--------------

constants.py provides three spectator permission switches:

SPECTATOR_CAN_EDIT_PRODUCTION
SPECTATOR_CAN_EDIT_RESEARCH
SPECTATOR_CAN_EDIT_APPEARANCE

All default to True.
Disable a switch to permit viewing without the corresponding editing ability.

Local spectators can use Annex and Delete below the selected country's faction controls.
Annex transfers another country's territory, divisions, and subjects immediately.
Delete requires confirmation. It removes the country's live state but preserves existing turn history.
Both actions are unavailable during turn processing, saving, map refresh, tactical play, and both multiplayer modes.
See documentation/diplo_stuff.txt for core cleanup, volunteer handling, and save compatibility.

Local spectators can open Puppets and select a country to release from its core land.
Back from the release panel returns to the country list. Cancel closes that list.
The release panel offers Integrated, Autonomous, and Independent. Each new panel starts with Integrated selected.
This control keeps the spectator's viewing identity. It does not permit tournament or real-time spectators to change another player's country.
See documentation/diplo_stuff.txt for release timing, Keep Cores, permissions, and save compatibility.

The editor's research screen sets a country's starting research.
Select a country in Map Research Editor to open its research timeline and category tabs.
Click an incomplete technology level to grant it and its prerequisites immediately.
This also grants earlier levels in the same technology.
For an OR requirement, keep a completed branch or add the branch that needs the fewest additional levels.

Click a completed level to remove it and later levels in the same technology.
Remove dependent levels only when their remaining prerequisites no longer permit them.
Another completed OR branch can preserve a dependent technology.
Clear changed research projects and progress that no longer meets prerequisites.
These controls change only the selected country. They do not change the map default.

In the map research editor, purple marks the exact default level when it is missing.
Pink marks that level when it is completed.
Earlier and later levels keep their normal colors.
Player and spectator research trees do not use these default colors.
The Completed tab is a text list. It has no technology edit buttons.

Edit ALL (Bulk) and Edit Map Default keep their separate Save controls.
Starting research editing requires the local map editor.
It is unavailable in tactical play, tournament multiplayer, and real-time multiplayer.

A spectator selects a country and sees its live research tree and queue.
Select View or Edit above the country search box before selecting a country.
View opens technology details without allowing research changes.
Edit previews technology changes, with the same prerequisite rules as the map editor.
The spectator research permission switch must permit Edit.
Tactical play and both multiplayer systems permit only View.

Select Exit or press the Back key to open the exit popup.
Select Save to apply the draft and leave the research screen.
Select Cancel to discard all edits and leave the research screen.
Select Go back to close the popup and keep the draft open.
The Back key and Escape also select Go back in this popup.
Draft changes do not change the country's saved research or progress.

An edit that discards active or paused research opens a Yes or No confirmation.
The message identifies the affected technologies and warns that progress will be lost.
Select No to keep the draft unchanged.
Select Yes to discard that progress in the draft.
Changing technologies back cannot restore this progress.
Cancel the entire edit to preserve the original research and progress.

Save checks permissions again and rejects changes if live research changed during editing.
A normal player sees their own research.
map_screen.viewing_research_country passes the selected country to the research screen.
map_screen.viewing_research_mode passes View or Edit. This temporary display choice is not saved.
editing_country serves the same purpose for the identity editor.

LAST-TURN UNIT EVENTS
---------------------

The yellow mail button beside the resources opens Unit events.
The orange screen lists unit names, tile IDs, events, health changes, and sources.
Each unit name has its country's flag on the left.
The flag uses the owner recorded in the event, including events for destroyed units.
Click a column header to sort the table.
Click a row to read its full details.

The Details column and expanded view show country flags before identified unit names.
Damage received and damage dealt identify all units involved, even when they are hidden.
Hidden units keep their names and flags, but their province number shows ???.

Use the mouse wheel or Page Up and Page Down to scroll long expanded details.
Flag images and detail text are prepared when the screen or expanded view opens.

Filter events appears to the left of Mark all unread.
Select one event type to show only matching entries.
Select No filter to show all permitted entries.
The button is orange with a selected filter and blue without one.
The selection stays when you reopen the report during the same turn.
Each completed turn clears the filter, including turns received by real-time clients.

The picker disables choices that would show no entries for the current viewer.
Available choices stay blue, including the selected event type.
Search keeps these states. Enter selects the first available search result.

The filter changes only the local display. Saves and multiplayer commands do not include it.
Opening the report and Mark all unread still affect all entries permitted for the current player.

The report records actual turn resolution, including losses and attacks by AI units.
AI plans and combat forecasts do not write entries.
Air combat entries use Interception in Details for interception damage and losses.
Strikes retain the Air missions label.
Damage dealt uses each source's share of the applied volley.
Shares include target restrictions and combat modifiers.
The total credit equals the target's actual health loss.
It excludes damage beyond the target's remaining health.
Forced destruction of grounded aircraft divides credit equally among the hostile units present.

Events also include destruction, disbanding, deployment, movement, repairs, upgrades, and transport or type changes.
Destroyed units remain in the report after removal from the map.
Normal players see their own units.
Tactical players see only their selected division, including its loss.
Normal spectators can see all recorded entries.
Editors and tournament spectators receive no unit reports.

The badge counts unread entries for the current player.
Opening the report marks those entries as read.
Click Mark all unread in the report to restore the current player's unread badge.
Closing the report keeps this unread status. Opening it again marks the entries as read.
Hotseat players have separate read status.
The next completed turn replaces the report and clears old read status.
Empty turns therefore remove earlier entries too.

Saves retain only the latest report and its local read status.
Older saves start with an empty report.
History snapshots do not store copies of reports.
Tournament archives retain all entries in the shared host and player snapshot.
The tournament report screen shows only the receiving player's entries.
Real-time snapshots include only the receiving player's entries.
Combat details hide unit positions with ??? when the viewer cannot see the unit.
Player move files and real-time commands cannot replace host reports.
Real-time broadcasts preserve local read status for the same turn.

Each new damage entry can include detail_parts with text fragments and identified units' recorded owners.
Saves, tournament exports, and real-time snapshots retain these fragments.
unit_events.normalize_log validates the fragments and preserves the original details text.
Old reports without detail_parts keep plain text for other units until the next completed turn.
Their main unit still has its recorded owner's flag.
Existing Unknown unit entries cannot recover identities omitted by older recording rules.

MAP DISPLAY
-----------

Map refreshes calculate layer inputs when game state changes.
They reuse pixel surfaces that have not changed.
Fog updates current visibility even when it can reuse its image.
Hotseat blanking and player changes still apply.

After all local players submit orders, the viewer becomes n/a while AI moves are prepared.
The AI preview must keep Resolve Turn, Settings, and Music available.
Country-owned actions remain hidden because no player country is assigned.
Hide Resolve Turn while AI preparation, map refresh, or saving is active.

Editor painting invalidates the affected surface.
Refresh Maps forces a full rebuild.
Planning-frame rendering does not run map-wide calculations.

Naval, Land, Air, and All appear beside the lower left sidebar in Units view.
They filter local unit stacks, army markers, and order arrows.
Hover previews list only visible units that match the selected category.
Training icons use the queued unit's category, even when the province has no deployed units.
Training icons do not depend on deployed unit boxes or compact markers.
Hidden or partially visible provinces do not show training icons.
Clicking a province still shows its full visible unit list.
The filter uses the unit's current form.
Convoys are naval, including transported aircraft. Trucks are land.
All is selected when a game opens.

Changing the filter clears local unit selection and compression animations.
It does not change units, orders, AI decisions, fog permissions, saves, or multiplayer commands.
Combat markers for matching units still estimate the complete battle.
Unknown fogged stacks and generic combat markers appear only in All.
Changing filters must not reveal hidden unit types.

Map boxes separate land, naval, and air units, including members of the same army.
Aircraft also separate by mission, including idle aircraft and each patrol priority.
Matching aircraft missions can share one rectangular box.
Active missions show a badge to the right of their box, sized like an army emblem.
Mission badges do not extend selection or hover hitboxes.
Tactical views show each visible division separately at every zoom.

At strategic zoom, markers combine aircraft missions within each domain and army.
Aircraft outside armies combine with nearby aircraft of the same owner.
Different armies remain separate.
Strategic zoom hides mission badges.

The army tray lists Unassigned first when Show Unclaimed is enabled and owned units remain outside saved army groups.
This fixed dark grey card has no symbol or editing controls.
It has no target area and gives no automatic orders.
Selecting it selects the player's units outside army groups.

These units keep their existing map boxes and markers without army symbols or color bands.
The card hides when all owned units belong to army groups, or Show Unclaimed is disabled in Settings.
Show Unclaimed is enabled by default and changes local display only.
Map state changes update its cached membership.

Mission badges use the same order visibility as map arrows.
The display cache refreshes when orders, unit forms, filters, or the viewer change.
