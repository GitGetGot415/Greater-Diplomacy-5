AIRCRAFT RULES
==============

AI means artificial intelligence. UI means user interface.
A wing is one aircraft unit in this guide.
A lane is a combat exchange between two hostile sides.
Each side can include several allied countries.

Unit capabilities
-----------------

The unit library owns these fields:

air_role
air_range_px
air_attack_multiplier
air_consumable
air_interception_immune
air_damages_forts

production_group is display metadata.
A missing air_damages_forts flag means the unit cannot damage forts.
Read capabilities from the current unit library, including for old saves.
Do not store them on individual units or accept them from multiplayer orders.

Range and display caches
------------------------

queries.build_air_geometry indexes actual ID-map pixels as horizontal runs when a map loads or is generated.
Each pixel has a closed rectangular footprint.
A target touching the range boundary counts as reachable.

Measure from the current base's map center to any part of the target tile.
Include disconnected parts of that tile.
Zoom, tilt, viewport, and horizontal display wrapping do not change reach.
Flight does not cross the display seam.

The geometry cache is temporary.
Rebuild it when map geometry changes.
Cache distances by base center and target.
Command validation checks only its target through air_target_in_range.
AI candidate validation must not repeat full-map scans.

Orders caches mission radii when input changes.
Draw one world-space circle for each distinct base and radius.
Apply camera tilt to its display.
Close the outline along map edges that lie inside the circle.
This keeps reach visible when the entire map fits inside the radius.

Cache rasterized outlines by base, radius, color, map dimensions, camera, viewport, and clipping state.
Air targeting replaces the movement outline with the strike outline.
Land bombardment retains tile markers.
Any tile part inside or touching the circle is in range.

Research details show library-based ranges, roles, damage bonuses, immunity, consumption, fort damage, and Convoy conversion.
Ground-combat roster stats are cached at selection and state changes.
They show immediate aircraft casualties without modifying units or assigning them front or reserve slots.

Flight and ground movement
--------------------------

R means the unit's listed air range.
Patrol and reusable strikes reach R.
Reusable repositioning reaches 2R.
One-use strikes reach R.

Aircraft can fly over water, foreign land, and nonadjacent provinces.
Reusable strikes reserve half their total travel distance for the outgoing flight and half for the return.
Thus, listed range equals strike reach.

Bases and landing tiles must be land.
Attack targets can be water.
Flight never captures territory.
Landed aircraft and their Convoy transports also cannot capture territory.
Loaded aircraft Convoys use normal naval transport movement.
The editor unit brush places aircraft only on land.

V1/V2 move between bases on legal land routes at their ground speed.
They cannot use AIR_REPOSITION or patrol.
Their ranged strike flies and consumes the weapon.

Mission controls
----------------

AIR_PATROL retains base_id and priority.
Old saves default to WEAKEST.

The air mission button offers four choices:
- No mission.
- Defend area, weakest enemy missions first.
- Defend area, strongest enemy missions first.
- Strike.

All choices remain visible.
Disable missions that the aircraft cannot perform.
Disable flight from an invalid launch base.

An available Defend area choice immediately issues AIR_PATROL with its selected priority.
Strike starts map targeting.
A valid target click issues AIR_ATTACK.
Clicking an active strike's mission button cancels it immediately.
No mission also cancels it.

Targeting accepts a left-click or the configured move-order click.
The default move-order click is right-click.
Consume that click in the strike or barrage selector.
It must not also issue a reposition order.
Dispatch each map event once, including the target click's release.

Patrol uses the listed mission radius.
This is half a reusable aircraft's movement radius.
Use the same tile-edge and disconnected-pixel range rules.
A normal move order replaces patrol with repositioning.
It does not extend the patrol area beyond its base range.

Button images show No Mission, Weakest First, Strongest First, or Strike Selected.
Relocation uses Air Move, including one-use weapons with a ground path.
Idle aircraft use No Mission.
Pending strike targeting uses Strike Selected.
Closing the selector without a choice changes nothing.

Choice callbacks recheck current unit identity, ownership, tactical control, and real-time planning permission.
The upgrade icon uses existing numbered-family upgrades.
Named aircraft do not upgrade into a different design.
Mission controls add no new save fields or network command kinds.

Group missions
--------------

Set Mission shows the number of eligible selected aircraft.
It always offers all four choices.
Each choice rechecks the original units, bases, permissions, and capabilities.

Group Strike selects one tile.
It assigns orders only to aircraft whose own range reaches the target.
Unsupported missions, stale units, and out-of-range targets keep their existing order and reserved payment.
Canceling group targeting also preserves previous orders.
Group actions use existing per-unit multiplayer commands.

Every reusable aircraft can patrol, including bombers and Zeppelin.
One-use weapons cannot patrol.
Unit data defines fighter air-to-air bonuses.

Resolution and transport
------------------------

AIR_ATTACK and AIR_REPOSITION retain base_id and target_id.
They resolve at the next turn boundary and clear once.
Reusable strikes keep the unit at its launch province.
Survivors retain damage there, which guarantees an exact return.
Patrol continues until replaced or canceled.

Aircraft can load only into Convoys.
Loading requires a coast. Unloading requires land.
Conversion uses the shared AIR_CONVERT_TURNS timer.
original_* transport fields preserve aircraft stats.
Loading replaces old infantry placeholder stats while preserving health ratios.

On load, legacy aircraft Trucks unpack on land or become Convoys at sea.
They retain identity and health fraction.
The AI moves V1/V2 on land.
It uses Convoys for reachable crossings when no land route is available.

Old Truck conversion orders become Convoy orders on a coast and cancel inland.
Keep normal land paths for V1/V2.
Discard those paths for reusable aircraft.

Interception
------------

All interceptable aircraft attacking one province form one committed force, including aircraft from different countries.
V2s form a separate interception-immune force.
Every hostile patrol covering the mission joins one shared coalition-lane battle.
It uses global COMBAT_WIDTH and the normal rules for three or more sides.

Rank missions by their committed calculate_unit_strength sums before losses.
Target IDs break ties.
Patrol priorities define ordering constraints between covered missions.
Use topological ordering when those constraints agree.
If they form a cycle, patrols vote for their next covered mission.
Most votes wins, then target ID.

Surviving patrols can fight several times.
Their effectiveness decreases with health.
Destroyed attackers do not deliver strikes.

Strikes and ground combat
-------------------------

After interception, surviving aircraft make one simultaneous exchange against the garrison.
Use shared coalition lanes, target-terrain combat width, front ranks, and reserves.
Defenders return fire even if that exchange destroys them.

Aircraft keep flight stats and Tanks-category immunity.
The fighter air-to-air bonus applies only during interception.
Health and political damage modifiers apply to both sides.
Fort defense protects only the garrison.
Flight never occupies or captures the target.

air_damages_forts controls fort damage.
Bombers, Zeppelin, V1 Flying Bomb, V2 Rocket, and Jet Fighters can damage forts.
Biplane and Monoplane Fighters cannot.
Each capable aircraft that reaches the target removes one fort level after the exchange.
This also applies to empty tiles.
Research unlock details show the capability.

Surviving reusable aircraft stay at their exact base with health and morale losses retained.
Remove destroyed aircraft.

A barrage or ranged strike against based aircraft uses shared target damage modifiers.
These include Tanks-category immunity, but exclude the fighter air-to-air bonus.

Hostile melee ground combat destroys aircraft immediately, before lane assignment or damage exchange.
This includes V1/V2 on land and aircraft unloaded from Convoys into hostile troops.
Neutral aircraft are unaffected.
Aircraft Convoys stay loaded during sea battles.
Non-mutating UI and AI estimates show the same casualties.
Hostile ground units at the launch base prevent aircraft from escaping through flight.

Multiplayer authority and visibility
-----------------------------------

Tournament files use authenticated aircraft_orders commands keyed by stable unit_id.
The host validates position, type, and range against its own state.
It retains its own health and stats.
Legacy snapshots provide only order and name drafts for existing aircraft.

New archives give each player an encrypted visibility projection and an opaque session context.
They never provide the host snapshot's decryption secret.
Legacy archives still load.

Real-time unit commands validate against server state.
Stable IDs resolve units when filtered client indexes differ.
Guest snapshots and initial map bundles remove hidden units and private foreign orders.
Neither multiplayer projection includes the temporary geometry cache or interception lists.
