DIPLOMACY RULES
===============

AI means artificial intelligence. UI means user interface.
A unilateral action needs no reply. A bilateral request needs a reply.

Map controls
------------

Closing the province panel cancels pending button presses immediately.
Country diplomacy controls require a current selection with an existing country owner.
If that selection is unavailable, the callback shows feedback and takes no action.
Replies from the message screen use their explicit country target without requiring a province selection.

Spectator country removal
------------------------

Local spectators see Annex and Delete below the country faction controls.
These actions are unavailable in the editor, tactical play, country selection, and both multiplayer modes.
They are also unavailable during turn processing, map refresh, or saving.
The game checks these permissions again when the spectator confirms an action.

Annex opens a country list. Select another country to annex it into the first selected country immediately.
All its territory and divisions transfer. This includes aircraft cargo and divisions in volunteer missions.
Its subjects transfer to the annexing country. The annexed country cannot remain in exile.
An Integrated annexer receives the territory directly. The game does not redirect it to its master.
Ordinary country cores remain. The existing cleanup rule removes cores of created Integrated puppets and active rebellions.

Delete requires confirmation. Cancel or press Back to keep the country.
Its territory becomes Unclaimed. The game removes its cores, divisions, production queues, and live country record.
It also clears live diplomacy references and releases its subjects.
Foreign volunteer divisions return to their owners. A surviving faction member can replace a deleted faction leader.
Existing turn history remains unchanged.

GLOBAL_EVENTS.deleted_countries stores deleted country IDs in saves and new history snapshots.
The template loader does not restore these IDs. Older saves without this list keep their existing behavior.
Loading a historical turn before deletion restores that turn's country state.
These controls do not add AI actions or multiplayer commands.

Shared processing and save data
------------------------------

map_logic/diplomacy/diplomacy_lifecycle.py owns request creation, replacement, removal, response binding, and timing transitions.
diplomacy_messages.py retains its public accessors and correspondence functions.
war_actions.py, puppet_actions.py, and faction_actions.py own their respective agreement actions.
diplomacy_agreements.py exports those actions for compatibility with older callers.
diplomacy_logic.py also exports agreement, processor, and message functions.
The processor retains its existing phase order. Treaty effects remain in treaty_effects.py and the agreement modules.
action_definitions.py declares action categories, availability handlers, and existing execution phases.
Controls, AI candidates, scripts, current multiplayer drafts, and send processing use shared availability.
Request cancellation precedes new-proposal checks. A changed leadership position cannot block cancellation of an unsent invitation.
Effect owners retain their existing resolution checks. AI preferences remain separate from gameplay availability.

RequestState gives explicit draft, scheduled, reply, reaction, and expired states.
The existing turns field remains the authoritative request age. State derives from that age, action, and schedule.
Responses remain independent records without travelling request counters.
Stable request references prevent an old response from resolving a replacement request.
Ratification uses a separate round reference and its existing member-answer rules.
See documentation/data_migrations.txt for saved identities and older-input adapters.

Friendly countries are faction members and members of the same imperial family.
There is no separate direct-alliance list or action.
See documentation/data_migrations.txt for older country records.

Simultaneous-action checks inspect country pairs named in current pending actions.
They preserve country insertion order.
One-way declarations remain included because they can cancel queued responses without a counter-proposal.
Each pair reads its actions again after earlier effects.
A removed proposal must not resolve from an old snapshot.
Unused country records do not add pairwise work.

Disk saves can store unused, unchanged catalog countries as empty {} records.
The loader restores their templates before diplomacy, faction repair, AI, or editor work.
Keep full records for countries referenced by provinces, cores, units, metadata, diplomacy, or scripted events.
Also keep full records for customized or landless countries, including governments in exile.
Placeholder keys preserve saved country order.

Tournament files, real-time snapshots, and turn history still contain complete country records.
Their authority and visibility rules remain unchanged.
See documentation/general_info.txt for disk history and tournament archive storage.

Message timing
--------------

The spectator message table shows three-letter month names with the first letter capitalized in the Date column.
This display format does not change stored dates or chronological sorting.

In the timelines below, 0.5 and 1.5 mean the send phase between planning turns.

A unilateral declaration needs no reply:

turn 0:   The sender writes the message.
turn 0.5: The message is sent.
turn 1:   The receiver gets the message.

A bilateral request needs a reply:

turn 0:   The sender writes the request.
turn 0.5: The request is sent.
turn 1:   The receiver gets it and writes an acceptance or rejection.
turn 1.5: The reply is sent.
turn 2:   The original sender gets the reply.

Allow cancellation of all outgoing diplomatic requests during the turn when they were written.
This includes war declarations and volunteer offers.

For faction joining, membership takes effect when the original sender receives acceptance at turn 2.

Crossing requests
-----------------

Different requests sent in opposite directions must not overwrite each other.

Example:

turn 0:   A offers B a trade. B asks A for military access.
turn 0.5: Both requests are sent.
turn 1:   A answers the access request. B answers the trade offer.
turn 1.5: Both replies are sent.
turn 2:   Each sender gets the reply to its own request.

Crossing requests can combine when acceptance would produce the same result.
Examples include a war invitation crossing a request to join that war.
A faction invitation can also cross a request to join that faction.

Military access is one-way.
A asking to enter B's land and B asking to enter A's land produce different results.
Keep those requests separate.

Guarantees of independence
-------------------------

Guarantee Independence is unilateral.
The guarantor stores the protected country in its own guarantees list.
The target cannot accept or reject the guarantee.
The target must be at peace and outside a faction.

Remove a guarantee as soon as those conditions cease to apply.
If an attack causes the change, first call the guarantors into the target's defensive war.
A country that started the war does not receive guarantee support.
The guarantor can revoke its own guarantee without a reply.

Military attach?s
-----------------

Military Attach? requests are bilateral.
The host must be at war and must not be fighting the sender.
After acceptance, the sender sees the host's battlefield visibility.

An AI host accepts valid requests unless the sender supports one of its enemies.
Support means active volunteer divisions or an attach? observing that enemy.
An accepted conflicting attach? is revoked on the following turn.
A player-controlled host can revoke a foreign attach? directly.

The attach? withdraws immediately if the host leaves its wars or becomes hostile to the sender.
The sender can also withdraw it without a reply.

Volunteer divisions
-------------------

Volunteers use the same support-conflict rule as attach?s.
AI hosts reject offers from countries with volunteers or attach?s at an enemy.
Existing conflicting volunteers return home on the following turn.
A player hosting foreign volunteers can send them home directly.

Aircraft are not eligible land volunteers.
This restriction uses the current unit library, including for aircraft with ground routes.
Aircraft remain ineligible as volunteers when transported in Convoys.
Units recovering from an air drop are also ineligible. See documentation/air_stuff.txt for recovery rules.

Editor and scripted events
--------------------------

The Global Diplomacy & Factions editor has a Diplomatic Agreements view.
It can set guarantees, attach?s at countries at war, and one-way military access.
These setup actions use the same rules and save fields as game actions.

`map_logic/ai/scripted_events.py` owns scripted conditions, actions, and the editor choices for them.
Scripted events can guarantee independence.
They can request attach?s or military access and offer volunteer divisions.
They can accept or reject attach? requests, access requests, and volunteer offers.

A volunteer offer uses its message value as the requested number of available land divisions.
A blank value requests one division.
It then uses the normal offer, acceptance, travel, and return process.

Offer Volunteer Divisions has a Use Available Capacity option.
This reduces the requested number to the remaining volunteer capacity.
It does not create an offer when capacity or eligible divisions are unavailable.

Event conditions can test:
- Guarantees.
- Military attach?s.
- One-way military access in either direction.
- Committed volunteer divisions.
- Remaining volunteer capacity.

Acceptance indicators
---------------------

Available bilateral proposals to AI countries show the shared deterministic acceptance verdict: YES or NO.
MAYBE is used for legal proposals to human-controlled targets, whose response cannot be predicted.
The indicator provides information only.
It does not change eligibility or queue a proposal.

When scripted events are enabled, forecasts also check matching Accept/Reject actions and their conditions.
This applies to outgoing diplomacy indicators and deal acceptance forecasts.
Random conditions produce an uncertain forecast.
UI rendering must not consume game randomness to predict those conditions.

Faction leadership
------------------

WorldState.factions owns stable faction identities, display names, leaders, ordered members, and faction pre-war borders.
faction_records.py derives country membership indexes and compatibility fields from those records.
Faction services use this boundary for membership, leadership, names, and disbanding.
Country-owned leadership pressure and tenure remain separate.
Renaming and succession preserve faction identity. Identity creation does not consume gameplay randomness.
Country pre-war borders remain separate from faction pre-war borders.
See documentation/data_migrations.txt for older saves, history, and snapshot projections.

Only the current faction leader can invite countries into an existing faction.
queries.can_invite_to_faction defines this restriction.
An ordinary member cannot send an invitation.
The game checks leadership when drafting, sending, and resolving an invitation.
A former leader can cancel an unsent invitation.
These rules also apply to scripted invitations, spectator invitation controls, and both multiplayer modes.
Editor faction setup can assign members directly.

The invitation button remains available to ordinary members when the other invitation conditions permit it.
Click it to see why only the leader can send the invitation.
Send a join request to the faction leader.
If you select an ordinary member, the join-request button explains that you must ask the leader.
Membership controls show NO when leadership prevents sending the request.
Invalid membership requests are not sent.

A member can challenge leadership after exceeding the leader's power by the required margin for six consecutive turns.
When several eligible claims resolve together, the claimant with the highest nation power becomes leader.
Country-data order does not decide the result.
Nation power combines military strength with one hundredth of economic power.
The peace table uses the same calculation.
An exact tie selects randomly among the strongest claimants.

When an AI member becomes leader, a generic "The country name Pact" name uses the new leader's country name.
Custom names remain unchanged.
Player-led leadership changes do not rename the faction.

Puppet hierarchy
----------------

A puppet can declare war or negotiate peace only with its master.
It cannot independently join, receive an invitation to, or leave a faction.
pull_puppets_into_faction moves puppets with their master.
queries.can_negotiate_peace and queries.can_choose_own_faction define these restrictions.
treaty_effects.apply_treaty_effect and faction_actions enforce them, including crossing invitations.

Puppet relationships must not contain a cycle.
The editor rejects any assignment that makes a country its own ancestor.
Shared puppet-state propagation visits each country at most once.
Malformed older scenario data must not cause endless recursion.

Release countries from cores
---------------------------

Players open Puppets, then Release Nation, to release countries from their own land.
Local spectators open Puppets and select the country whose land supplies the release.
This does not change the spectator's viewing identity.
Back from the release panel returns to the country list. Cancel closes the country list.
Tournament and real-time spectators cannot release countries for other players.
Tactical players and map editors cannot use these release controls.

The release panel offers Integrated, Autonomous, and Independent above Keep Cores.
Each new panel starts with Integrated selected.
Each queued release stores its selected type. Later control changes do not change queued releases.
Queued release text is yellow for Integrated, green for Autonomous, and light blue for Independent.
Cancel a queued release before changing its options.

All choices use the same core-territory rule and the existing country creation rules.
Keep Cores excludes tiles with the releasing country's core.
Earlier queued releases take overlapping land first.
Each release must have eligible land when queued. Check eligible land again when the release resolves.
Do not create a country if no eligible land remains.

Integrated and Autonomous countries become subjects through the shared puppet assignment rule.
They join the master's faction and wars under that rule.
Independent countries have no master. They do not inherit the releasing country's faction or wars.
Integrated releases use the master's color and adjective in the generated name.
Autonomous and Independent releases keep the source country's name, adjective, and color.
Add a number to the generated name when it conflicts with an existing country.
All three choices inherit the releasing country's research.

release_puppet_queue entries store core_nation, keep_cores, release_type, and turns_left.
The save loader preserves these nested queue fields.
New releases resolve after one turn. Existing countdowns remain authoritative.

Tournament move files carry explicit release_subjects requests.
The host validates the source country, type, core land, duplicates, and unchanged options.
It preserves session checks, turn checks, and host countdowns.
Real-time clients submit release choices in puppet_draft. Only the server applies and resolves those commands.
Neither protocol accepts a client-supplied countdown for a new release.

Government in exile
-------------------

Air cargo keeps its owner during flight. It still counts toward the owner's land division total.
Annexation and tactical independence transfer a carrier and its cargo to the same country.

A faction member that loses its last province remains in the faction as a government in exile.
It keeps its units, faction wars, diplomacy, leadership, and faction pre-war border record.
Exile is derived from faction membership and lack of territory.
Regaining a province ends exile immediately.

Normal faction capture transfers can restore a member's core or pre-war territory.
If all faction members become landless, disband the faction immediately.
Remove its historical border record.
Apply ordinary country cleanup to every former member.
No country remains at war with a faction that cannot restore territory.

A landless country outside a faction uses ordinary cleanup.
Ordinary cleanup removes wars and pending offers.
A player-created Integrated puppet also loses its record and cores.
A rebellion defeated while still at war loses its record and cores.
A scenario's Integrated puppet keeps its cores after annexation.
