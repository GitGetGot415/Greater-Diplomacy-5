PLAYER CONTROLS AND PERMISSIONS
===============================

SCOPE
-----

This guide covers player actions, permissions, and visible results.
Update an existing topic only when one of these changes.
Keep styling constants, asset filenames, cache internals, and development change reports out of this guide.
See documentation/ui_reference.txt for UI configuration and artwork.
See documentation/general_info.txt for state ownership, storage, and presentation caches.
See documentation/data_migrations.txt for older-data compatibility.

AI means artificial intelligence. UI means user interface. SFX means sound effects.
See documentation/non_llm_ai.txt for AI decisions and shared combat rules.
See documentation/diplo_stuff.txt for diplomacy controls, timing, and effects.
See documentation/air_stuff.txt for aircraft controls and rules.

DISPLAY AND SOUND SETTINGS
--------------------------

Music, album, and song buttons use the SFX volume and pitch settings on desktop and web builds.
Select Dithering in Edit Portrait to toggle square transitions. The preview updates immediately.
The choice is saved. Older settings keep dithering enabled.
Dithering is disabled when Gradient or Stepped is off. Its saved choice remains unchanged.

Use Checkerboard Gradient in Settings to adjust square shading.
The default, 0, gives flat fills. Use 1 for button shading or up to 2 for more contrast.
Square bevels stay unchanged.
Use Checkerboard Speed to set movement from 0 to 100 pixels per second. Zero stops movement.
The speed applies to menu backgrounds, map sky, and checkerboard water.
The game saves this setting. Reset Defaults restores the default speed.
Checkerboard Water mirrors the sky pattern across the map horizon.
The pattern follows map panning, zoom, and tilt.
Zero tilt gives no vertical compression. Full tilt gives full vertical compression.

COUNTRY SUMMARY
---------------

Hover over the flag in the top left to highlight it.
Click the flag to open that country's summary.
Select another country's land tile to show its flag and summary.
Selecting water keeps the current player's flag.
The summary is read-only. Back returns to the same player and selected tile.

Long summaries support the mouse wheel, scrollbar, Page Up, and Page Down.

Military shows the combined attack of available units with their current health and national damage modifiers.
Combat width, missions, and target defenses can change actual damage.

Foreign military figures include only visible units. They do not reveal hidden cargo.
Strategic players see their complete army. Tactical players keep their restricted battlefield visibility.
Local spectators and editors can see complete armies.

Politics shows the political position, direction, research modifier, and policy states.
Diplomacy shows the faction, leader, members, overlord, puppets, and enemies.

Economy shows stockpiles, income, expenses, and net income when the viewer can inspect private economics.
Text shows conscription and material conversion. The summary has no sliders.
Private economies hide these values as well as resource figures.
Players can inspect their own economy.
Faction members, direct puppets, and the overlord share economy figures in all game modes.
Other foreign economies require disabled fog, spectator mode, or editor mode in local games.
Other foreign economies remain private in both multiplayer systems.
Real-time clients receive shared totals from the server without private orders or research plans.
The Tournament Spectator cannot inspect private economics.

Flag inspection is unavailable during country selection, hidden UI, turn processing, saving, and blocking confirmations.

BATTLE CONTROLS
---------------

Battle replaces Give Orders on a tile where combat is active.
Battle columns belong to combat sides, which can include several countries.
The reserve column also shows allied units that the player cannot edit.
Those units can replace losses in the front rank.
See documentation/non_llm_ai.txt, LANE COMBAT, for combat rules and order permissions.

TACTICAL PLAY
-------------

A tactical player commands one division.
The host country remains AI-controlled.

- The AI cannot disband the player's division.
- Orders and Battle allow edits only to that division.
  They still display other country units.
- Diplomatic popups for the host's inbox do not interrupt tactical play.
  Old host messages do not appear after the player declares independence.

Declaring independence ends tactical mode.

SPECTATOR PLAY
--------------

Spectators can edit production, research, and country appearance when the corresponding permission is enabled.
These permissions are enabled by default. Disabling a permission permits viewing without editing.
See documentation/general_info.txt, SPECTATOR PERMISSION SETTINGS, for the switches.

See documentation/diplo_stuff.txt for spectator annexation, deletion, and releases from cores.

RESEARCH OUTPUT BONUSES
-----------------------

Research details list each output bonus and its affected building families or groups.
Building yields include the selected country's completed output research.
Production previews also include the selected province's territory penalty.
See documentation/general_info.txt, BUILDING OUTPUT RESEARCH, for stacking, saved research, and data sources.

RESEARCH EDITING
----------------

The Infantry category uses named equipment stages for infantry, cavalry, militia, and artillery.
Each stage has numbered upgrades. Vehicle unlocks appear on a separate row below cavalry.
Technology details show the units unlocked by each stage.
See documentation/non_llm_ai.txt, HISTORICAL INFANTRY EQUIPMENT, for shared equipment and upgrade rules.

The editor's research screen sets a country's starting research.
Select a country in Map Research Editor to open its research timeline and category tabs.
Click an incomplete technology level to grant it and its prerequisites immediately.
This also grants earlier levels in the same technology.
For an OR requirement, keep a completed branch or add the branch that needs the fewest additional levels.

Click a completed level to remove it and later levels in the same technology.
Remove dependent levels only when their remaining prerequisites no longer permit them.
Another completed OR branch can preserve a dependent technology.
Clear changed research projects and progress that no longer meets prerequisites.
These controls change only the selected country. They do not change the map default.

In the map research editor, purple marks the exact default level when it is missing.
Pink marks that level when it is completed.
Earlier and later levels keep their normal colors.
Player and spectator research trees do not use these default colors.
The Completed tab is a text list. It has no technology edit buttons.

Edit ALL (Bulk) and Edit Map Default keep their separate Save controls.
Starting research editing requires the local map editor.
It is unavailable in tactical play, tournament multiplayer, and real-time multiplayer.

A spectator selects a country and sees its live research tree and queue.
Select View or Edit above the country search box before selecting a country.
View opens technology details without allowing research changes.
Edit previews technology changes, with the same prerequisite rules as the map editor.
The spectator research permission switch must permit Edit.
Tactical play and both multiplayer systems permit only View.

Select Exit or press the Back key to open the exit popup.
Select Save to apply the draft and leave the research screen.
Select Cancel to discard all edits and leave the research screen.
Select Go back to close the popup and keep the draft open.
The Back key and Escape also select Go back in this popup.
Draft changes do not change the country's saved research or progress.

An edit that discards active or paused research opens a Yes or No confirmation.
The message identifies the affected technologies and warns that progress will be lost.
Select No to keep the draft unchanged.
Select Yes to discard that progress in the draft.
Changing technologies back cannot restore this progress.
Cancel the entire edit to preserve the original research and progress.

Save checks permissions again and rejects changes if live research changed during editing.
A normal player sees their own research.

LAST-TURN UNIT EVENTS
---------------------

The yellow mail button beside the resources opens Unit events.
The orange screen lists unit names, tile IDs, events, health changes, and sources.
Each unit name has its country's flag on the left.
The flag uses the owner recorded in the event, including events for destroyed units.
Click a column header to sort the table.
Click a row to read its full details.

The Details column and expanded view show country flags before identified unit names.
Damage received and damage dealt identify all units involved, even when they are hidden.
Hidden units keep their names and flags, but their province number shows ???.

Use the mouse wheel or Page Up and Page Down to scroll long expanded details.

Filter events appears to the left of Mark all unread.
Select one event type to show only matching entries.
Select No filter to show all permitted entries.
The button is orange with a selected filter and blue without one.
The selection stays when you reopen the report during the same turn.
Each completed turn clears the filter, including turns received by real-time clients.

The picker disables choices that would show no entries for the current viewer.
Available choices stay blue, including the selected event type.
Search keeps these states. Enter selects the first available search result.

The filter is local and lasts only for the current turn.
Opening the report and Mark all unread affect all permitted entries, including entries hidden by the filter.

Events record actual turn results, including damage, destruction, disbanding, deployment, movement, repairs, upgrades, and transport or type changes.
Damage credit includes combat modifiers and target restrictions, but excludes damage beyond the target's remaining health.
Destroyed units remain in the report after removal from the map.

Normal players see their own units.
Tactical players see only their selected division, including its loss.
Normal spectators can see all recorded entries.
Editors and tournament spectators receive no unit reports.
Both multiplayer systems show only the receiving player's entries.

The badge counts unread entries for the current player.
Opening the report marks those entries as read.
Click Mark all unread in the report to restore the current player's unread badge.
Closing the report keeps this unread status. Opening it again marks the entries as read.
Hotseat players have separate read status.
The next completed turn replaces the report and clears old read status.
Empty turns therefore remove earlier entries too.

Saves retain the latest report and read status. History does not retain reports.
Older reports can lack flags for other units or show Unknown unit until the next completed turn.

MAP DISPLAY
-----------

Display defaults:

- Strategic country selection: Political layer, country names on, Units off.
- Tactical country selection and play: Units on, country names off.
- Normal strategic play and loaded games: start with Units on and country names off.

Tactical rendering draws every visible division at every zoom.
Do not apply strategic fading or zoomed-out army compression.
In tactical country selection, a clicked unit box selects from that box's divisions.
Fall back to the tile only when no rendered box exists there.

After all local players submit orders, no player country is assigned while AI moves are prepared.
The AI preview keeps Resolve Turn, Settings, and Music available.
Country-owned actions remain hidden because no player country is assigned.
Resolve Turn is hidden while AI preparation, map refresh, or saving is active.

Select Refresh Maps to rebuild all map layers.

In Economy view, factory icons turn gray while their province has units in production.
The province hover shows building output with the owner's research bonuses and territory penalties.
Factories without queued units keep their normal colors.
Partial fog keeps the existing unknown building marker.
The Units view filter does not affect this production display.

Naval, Land, Air, and All form a vertical strip beside the lower left sidebar in Units view.
Switching to another map view slides the filter buttons and their background closed before they disappear.
Returning to Units view opens the strip unless you used Hide to close it.
They filter local unit stacks, army markers, and order arrows.
Hover previews list only visible units that match the selected category.
Training icons use the queued unit's category, even when the province has no deployed units.
Training icons do not depend on deployed unit boxes or compact markers.
Hidden or partially visible provinces do not show training icons.

Clicking a province still shows its full visible unit list.
The filter uses the unit's current form.
Convoys are naval, including transported aircraft. Trucks are land.

Click Hide above the strip to hide its buttons and background. The toggle moves down to the bottom-left controls.
Click Show to open the strip again. The toggle moves back above the strip.
Outside Units view, this button stays visible without text and is disabled.
The strip slides open or closed.
The filter buttons cannot be selected while the strip moves.
The selected filter stays active while the strip is hidden.
Each new map session starts with All selected and the strip open.
The filter and strip visibility are local to the current map session.

Changing the filter clears local unit selection and compression animations.
Combat markers for matching units still estimate the complete battle.
Unknown fogged stacks and generic combat markers appear only in All.
Changing filters must not reveal hidden unit types.

The bubble color uses the predicted winner among countries that participate in combat.
Countries outside the combat lanes cannot determine the color, regardless of their health.
The forecast ranks surviving participants by health, then attack, then speed.
An unresolved forecast or a battle without surviving participants uses grey.

Map boxes separate land, naval, and air units, including members of the same army.
See documentation/air_stuff.txt, Mission controls, for aircraft grouping, mission badges, and strategic markers.

ARMY GROUPS
-----------

The army list starts with Unassigned when Show Unassigned is enabled and owned units remain outside army groups.
It includes all owned unit types, including units on foreign land.
Settings places Show Unassigned between Show FPS and Show Tutorial Popup.
Show Unassigned is enabled by default, including in older settings files.
Reset Defaults enables it again.

The card is dark grey and has no symbol.
Click the card to select its units.
The card cannot be edited, removed, moved, or given a target area.
It gives no automatic orders and does not change army membership.

Right-click assignment applies only to saved army groups.
Unassigned units keep their normal map display, without army symbols or color bands.

In the army editor, click the Name field before typing or using name clipboard shortcuts.
A white border and text cursor show that the field is active.
Click elsewhere to stop editing the name. Long names scroll within the active field to keep the text cursor visible.
Enter still saves the editor draft. Escape closes the editor without saving.

MAP EDITOR PAINTING
-------------------

Brush and Paint buttons sit between Nation Brush and Load.
These buttons apply only to territory and cores. Other tools keep their existing behavior.
Brush changes tiles during a drag. Each editor session starts with Brush selected.
Paint changes one connected land region per click. Water tiles stop the fill.
Territory Paint matches the clicked tile's owner. Core Paint matches its complete core set, regardless of list order.
Left-click applies the selected nation. Unclaimed clears cores.
Right-click removes the selected nation's cores, or picks a territory owner.
Undo and Redo apply to the complete fill. The selected tool is not saved.

MAP EDITOR METADATA
-------------------

The local map editor shows Metadata in place of Mail.
It contains Scenario Name, Author, and Description text fields.
Select Apply Metadata to keep changes in the current map. Back closes the draft without applying it.
Use Save on the map to write the scenario and its metadata to disk.
Metadata changes require an idle local editor. Tournament, real-time, tactical, and ordinary player sessions cannot apply these changes.

Scenario Name comes from the loaded scenario's folder name. New editor maps start with an empty name.
Changing Scenario Name changes the next save's destination. It does not rename or delete the original folder.
Older scenarios without metadata open with empty Author and Description fields.

A named scenario saves directly under that name in the custom scenarios directory.
An empty name uses the existing MapExport_ name with a date and time.
The name must be one valid folder name. Path separators and reserved system names are not permitted.
If the destination exists, choose Overwrite, Save Copy, or Cancel.
Overwrite replaces the scenario files in that folder.
Save Copy selects the first unused numbered name, such as Example-1 or Example-2.
Cancel and Back leave existing files unchanged.
After a save, Metadata shows the actual destination folder's name.
Browser builds also keep custom scenarios in persistent storage.

MAP EDITOR BULK CHANGES
-----------------------

Select the square blue Bulk Edit button in the editor's bottom toolbar.
Select Clear Map Items inside Bulk Edit to open the existing clearing tools.
These tools require an idle local map editor.
They are unavailable in player games, tactical play, tournament multiplayer, and real-time multiplayer.
Each tool requires confirmation before it changes the map.

Set All Buildings opens a choice of Factories, Recruitment Centers, or Forts.
Select a building type, then select its target level from the active building library.
Each action changes only existing buildings of the selected type.
For example, setting forts changes only tiles that already have a fort.
It does not add the selected building type to other tiles.
Other buildings and construction queues stay unchanged.
This can increase or decrease building levels. Map Undo and Redo can reverse the change.

Set Army by Research Year selects the latest equipment available by the entered year for each unit family.
The selection uses equipment metadata and the active research timeline.
A unit stays unchanged when its family has no equipment available by that year.

The army tool changes placed ground, naval, and air units, including transported units and aircraft cargo.
It preserves unit identities, owners, custom names, army assignments, and health percentages.
It clears orders on changed units. It does not change research, production queues, or off-map volunteer equipment.
Map Undo and Redo can reverse these equipment changes.

Reset All Diplomacy ends wars and removes factions, puppet links, guarantees, military access, and military attaches.
It clears relation modifiers, truces, diplomatic cooldowns, pending requests, replies, treaty ratifications, and diplomatic return or release queues.
Volunteer divisions return to their owners immediately.
Territory, cores, country appearance, research, and message history stay unchanged.
Resources held for pending offers or treaties return to their country's stockpiles.
Territorial claims and cores can still affect relation scores. Scripted events can establish new agreements later.
The reset clears Map Undo and Redo history. Save a copy before resetting diplomacy if you need the current setup.

Data Refresh requires confirmation before it reloads country defaults and synchronizes unit statistics.
The confirmation lists the country appearance fields that the refresh resets.
Cancel or press Back to keep the current data.
