diplomacy requirements

unilateral declarations:
turn 0: msg is written
turn 0.5: msg is sent
turn 1: msg is received

declarations requiring response:
turn 0: msg1 is written
turn 0.5: msg1 is sent
turn 1: msg1 is received, receiver writes msg2 back
turn 1.5: msg2 is sent
turn 2: msg2 is received by original sender

maybe add testcases for each potential diplomatic action?
each test case should return how long it takes for each statement to send and what happens, one example could be of a faction join request, returning something simmilar to this:

turn 0: [A] writes request to join faction of [B]
turn 0.5: request message is sent
turn 1: [B] receives request message from [A], accepts request and sends acceptance back to [A]
turn 1.5: acceptance message is sent
turn 2: [A] receives acceptance message, [A] is now officially accepted into the faction

if two bilateral requests are sent to each other, they shouldn't overwrite each other
you SHOULD get something like this:

turn 0: [A] writes trade deal to country [B]
turn 0: [B] request millitary access from country [A]
turn 0.5: both messages are sent
turn 1: [A] receives the millitary access request from [B], either accepts or declines it
turn 1: [B] receives the trade deal from [A], either accepts it or declines it
turn 1.5: both messages are sent
turn 2: [A] receives acceptance or declining of trade deal, [B] receives acceptance or declining of millitary access

if something else happens, like one of these messages getting overriden, that shouldn't be the case

the one exception though is if both are requesting something that if accepted will lead to the same outcome

for example, [A] asking [B] to join their war, and [B] asking [A] if they can join in their war
another example, [A] inviting [B] to their faction, and [B] requesting to join the faction of [A]

however, do keep in mind that things like [A] requesting millitary access from [B] and [B] requesting millitary access from [A] don't lead to the same outcome because millitary access is a one way thing
it's possible for a country to let another country into it's borders but also not be allowed into the other countries borders
