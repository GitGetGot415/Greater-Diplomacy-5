HOW THE AI WORKS
================

Two layers. The heuristic layer decides everything and runs always, with or
without a language model. The model layer, when switched on, chooses between
options the heuristic layer has already built and judged legal.

That order matters: the model never invents an action, names a country, or
starts a war the rules forbid, because it only ever picks from a list that was
built by the ordinary rules. Turn the model off and the game plays the same way,
just without the prose and the summits.


IMMERSION LEVELS (Settings -> the red buttons)
----------------------------------------------

OFF       the model is never called; everything answers from the canned table

LITE      the model is off unless a human writes a custom message, in which case
          a reply is generated for it

FULL      the model handles anything a human is involved in. AI talking to AI
          stays canned, since nobody would read it

MAJOR     the model additionally drives the countries a player actually notices:
          the strongest handful, anyone at war with or bordering a human, and
          faction leaders. On the 1939 map that is about 8 nations of 36.
          Great-power diplomacy without ABSOLUTE's cost -- this is the one to
          recommend to most people

ABSOLUTE  every AI nation is model-driven, and AI nations hold summits with each
          other. Slow, and the reason to run it is that the world starts making
          agreements you were never part of and can read about afterwards

Per country, nation_data["llm_tier"] of ALWAYS / NEVER / AUTO overrides the
level, so a player can make one rival model-driven without paying for forty.


WHAT THE MODEL ACTUALLY DECIDES
-------------------------------

1. Which action a nation takes. Once per nation per turn it receives that
   nation's whole menu of legal moves -- scored, with reasons -- and picks up to
   three. The same reply supplies the wording, so this replaced the old
   per-message text pass rather than adding to it. A nation with nothing to
   choose between is not asked at all.

2. Whether to accept a proposal, when the call is marginal. A structural rule
   (an integrated puppet cannot trade) is stated as settled; a judgement call
   arrives as a recommendation with its reasoning and can be overruled.

3. What is agreed at a summit (ABSOLUTE, or MAJOR between two major powers).

Anything it asks for that the rules do not allow is dropped, with a rumour in
the global log so the pressure is still visible.


THE HEURISTIC LAYER
-------------------

map_logic/ai/
  ai_world.py        one snapshot of the map per turn; every strength, border
                     and relation query answers from it
  ai_unit_eval.py    what a unit is worth to a nation, from its stats and that
                     nation's own scarcity. There is no list of good units
  ai_tech_eval.py    what a tech is worth, from what it unlocks
  ai_personality.py  five traits per nation, seeded from the scenario so a save
                     is reproducible. Editable: map editor -> AI Personality
  ai_opinion.py      war, peace, trade and alliance appetite as degrees
  ai_candidates.py   the menu of legal moves, and the arbiter that ranks it
  ai_commitments.py  promises between nations, and what breaking one costs
  ai_negotiation.py  summits
  ai_director.py     the model choosing from the menu
  ai_construction.py, ai_movement.py, ai_research.py, ai_diplomacy.py


TWO THINGS THE VALUATIONS GET RIGHT THAT ARE EASY TO GET WRONG
---------------------------------------------------------------

An extra body is worth what it ABSORBS, not a flat bonus. apply_group_damage
deals sum(max(0, volley/N - defense)), so at defense 0 that sum is exactly the
volley whatever N is -- splitting a stack into more zero-defense bodies absorbs
nothing at all. Crediting every body equally made the cheapest unit in the game
the best buy for any nation with spare manpower, which is how a country ends up
with eighty militia and no infantry.

A building tech is worth what it ADDS. Upgrading a factory deletes the level
below it and costs a flat 30,000 at every tier, so Lvl 25 gains exactly what
Lvl 2 does. Scoring the new building's whole output overstated it by a factor
of the level, which made unreachable tiers look like the best deals on the
board. And a level further ahead than the nation has actually built is worth
nothing, because a province can only ever queue the next item in its chain.


TUNING
------

data/constants.py, the AI blocks. The ones worth knowing:

  AI_WAR_DESIRE_THRESHOLD     how keen a nation must be to declare war
  AI_LINE_SPEND_RATIO         bodies versus firepower, as a share of spending
  AI_W_SOAK                   what one more body is worth, scaled by the share
                              of a hit its defense actually absorbs
  AI_TECH_ECONOMY_WEIGHT      industry versus army in research
  AI_BUILDING_PAYBACK_TURNS   how long a building gets to earn back its cost.
                              Separate from AI_TECH_ECONOMY_HORIZON, which
                              scales income against an army -- sharing one
                              number made every building in the game a loss
  AI_MAX_BUILDING_RESEARCH_LEAD   levels of building tech the AI will research
                              ahead of what it has built
  AI_COMMITMENT_WEIGHT        how much a pact restrains, before loyalty
  AI_MAX_ACTIONS_PER_TURN     proposals a nation may have in flight
  AI_NEGOTIATION_MAX_PAIRS    summits per turn
  ai_turn_budget_seconds      (a setting) wall-clock ceiling per turn's model
                              work; whatever has not answered falls back

Personalities are edited in the map editor under AI Personality, which pins
them so nothing regenerates them. A scenario can also pin them by hand:
  "ai_personality_overrides": {"German Reich": {"aggression": 0.9}}
Traits are aggression, ambition, caution, loyalty, trust, all 0.0 to 1.0.
Without either they are procedural, which means reproducible but historically
arbitrary -- this seed gives the 1939 German Reich 0.51 aggression and the
USSR 0.04, so the historical scenarios are worth authoring.


RULES WORTH KNOWING
-------------------

Puppets cannot settle their own wars, only wars against their own master --
the mirror of the rule that they may only declare war on their master. The
backstop is in treaty_effects.apply_treaty_effect, where every path converges.

Bombardment is free, draws no return fire, ignores the combat-width cap, and
a bombarding unit still fights on its own tile. The AI shells whenever it has
a target in range, which is also what stops artillery charging into melee.

Multi-turn orders (DISBAND, CONVERT, REPAIR) survive the AI's per-turn
movement re-plan. They did not used to, which meant no AI unit was ever
disbanded by anything.
