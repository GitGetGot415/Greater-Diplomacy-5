HOW THE AI WORKS
================

Two layers. The heuristic layer decides everything and runs always, with or
without a language model. The model layer, when switched on, chooses between
options the heuristic layer has already built and judged legal.

That order matters: the model never invents an action, names a country, or
starts a war the rules forbid, because it only ever picks from a list that was
built by the ordinary rules. Turn the model off and the game plays the same way,
just without the prose and the summits.


IMMERSION LEVELS (Settings -> the red buttons)
----------------------------------------------

OFF       the model is never called; everything answers from the canned table

LITE      the model is off unless a human writes a custom message, in which case
          a reply is generated for it

FULL      the model handles anything a human is involved in. AI talking to AI
          stays canned, since nobody would read it

MAJOR     the model additionally drives the countries a player actually notices:
          the strongest handful, anyone at war with or bordering a human, and
          faction leaders. On the 1939 map that is about 8 nations of 36.
          Great-power diplomacy without ABSOLUTE's cost -- this is the one to
          recommend to most people

ABSOLUTE  every AI nation is model-driven, and AI nations hold summits with each
          other. Slow, and the reason to run it is that the world starts making
          agreements you were never part of and can read about afterwards

Per country, nation_data["llm_tier"] of ALWAYS / NEVER / AUTO overrides the
level, so a player can make one rival model-driven without paying for forty.


WHAT THE MODEL ACTUALLY DECIDES
-------------------------------

1. Which action a nation takes. Once per nation per turn it receives that
   nation's whole menu of legal moves -- scored, with reasons -- and picks up to
   three. The same reply supplies the wording, so this replaced the old
   per-message text pass rather than adding to it. A nation with nothing to
   choose between is not asked at all.

2. Whether to accept a proposal, when the call is marginal. A structural rule
   (an integrated puppet cannot trade) is stated as settled; a judgement call
   arrives as a recommendation with its reasoning and can be overruled.

3. What is agreed at a summit (ABSOLUTE, or MAJOR between two major powers).

Anything it asks for that the rules do not allow is dropped, with a rumour in
the global log so the pressure is still visible.


THE HEURISTIC LAYER
-------------------

map_logic/ai/
  ai_world.py        one snapshot of the map per turn; every strength, border
                     and relation query answers from it
  ai_unit_eval.py    what a unit is worth to a nation, from its stats and that
                     nation's own scarcity. There is no list of good units
  ai_tech_eval.py    what a tech is worth, from what it unlocks
  ai_personality.py  five traits per nation, seeded from the scenario so a save
                     is reproducible. Editable: map editor -> AI Personality
  ai_opinion.py      war, peace, trade and alliance appetite as degrees
  ai_candidates.py   the menu of legal moves, and the arbiter that ranks it
  ai_commitments.py  promises between nations, and what breaking one costs
  ai_negotiation.py  summits
  ai_director.py     the model choosing from the menu
  ai_construction.py, ai_movement.py, ai_research.py, ai_diplomacy.py


THREE THINGS THE VALUATIONS GET RIGHT THAT ARE EASY TO GET WRONG
----------------------------------------------------------------

An extra body is worth what it ABSORBS, not a flat bonus. apply_group_damage
deals sum(max(0, volley/N - defense)), so at defense 0 that sum is exactly the
volley whatever N is -- splitting a stack into more zero-defense bodies absorbs
nothing at all. Crediting every body equally made the cheapest unit in the game
the best buy for any nation with spare manpower, which is how a country ends up
with eighty militia and no infantry.

A building tech is worth what it ADDS. Upgrading a factory deletes the level
below it and costs a flat 30,000 at every tier, so Lvl 25 gains exactly what
Lvl 2 does. Scoring the new building's whole output overstated it by a factor
of the level, which made unreachable tiers look like the best deals on the
board. And a level further ahead than the nation has actually built is worth
nothing, because a province can only ever queue the next item in its chain.

BUY AND SELL BY THE SAME VALUATION. The purchase loop takes the argmax of
ai_unit_eval's marginal score; the disband pass used to take the minimum raw
attack, and the two disagreed -- Japan, the German Reich and France were each
paying for one unit type and scrapping that same type in the same turn. If a
second place in the code has to decide what a unit is worth, it asks
ai_unit_eval, or the AI argues with itself.


TUNING
------

data/constants.py, the AI blocks. The ones worth knowing:

  AI_WAR_DESIRE_THRESHOLD     how keen a nation must be to declare war
  AI_LINE_SPEND_RATIO         bodies versus firepower, as a share of spending
  AI_W_SOAK                   what one more body is worth, scaled by the share
                              of a hit its defense actually absorbs
  AI_TECH_ECONOMY_WEIGHT      industry versus army in research
  AI_BUILDING_PAYBACK_TURNS   how long a building gets to earn back its cost.
                              Separate from AI_TECH_ECONOMY_HORIZON, which
                              scales income against an army -- sharing one
                              number made every building in the game a loss
  AI_MAX_BUILDING_RESEARCH_LEAD   levels of building tech the AI will research
                              ahead of what it has built
  AI_COMMITMENT_WEIGHT        how much a pact restrains, before loyalty
  AI_MAX_ACTIONS_PER_TURN     proposals a nation may have in flight
  AI_NEGOTIATION_MAX_PAIRS    summits per turn
  AI_UPKEEP_TARGETS           share of each resource's income upkeep may eat
                              before the AI stops buying more of what needs it.
                              The fuel entry is the lever if armies come out
                              too small or too large -- UPKEEP_MODIFIERS["fuel"]
                              is 0.50 against 0.05 for materials, so fuel is
                              what actually binds
  MAX_COMBAT_ATTACKERS        combat width. Not an AI setting, but the AI reads
                              it in nine places, so raising it buys roughly
                              proportionally more assault units per frontline
                              tile and lets fronts hold proportionally more.
                              Source-only: no scenario field, no editor field
  AI_REINFORCE_COMBAT_WEIGHT  how hard a battle pulls units in, at full
                              strength on an empty tile and fading to nothing
                              as the firing line fills
  AI_REAR_GUARD_PENALTY       how hard a quiet border pushes units away once one
                              is standing there. The guard stays at one whatever
                              the combat width is -- a quiet border is a
                              garrison, not a firing line
  ai_turn_budget_seconds      (a setting) wall-clock ceiling on model work for
                              a whole turn; whatever has not answered falls
                              back. Opened once by prepare_turn, and the three
                              phases that spend it draw shares from the same
                              clock
  AI_BUDGET_SHARE_SUMMITS     what each phase may take of whatever the turn has
  AI_BUDGET_SHARE_DIRECTOR    left when it starts, in the order they run. A
  AI_BUDGET_SHARE_RESPONSES   phase that finishes early hands the rest forward;
                              the last takes the remainder
  ai_threads                  (a setting) how many model calls are in flight at
                              once. 1 by default, which against a local model
                              means one nation per call in series

Personalities are edited in the map editor under AI Personality -- the purple
button on the left bar, which pins them so nothing regenerates them. It spent
its first release invisible, drawn underneath the Claims button on the same
row; test_editor_menu_layout now refuses to let two buttons share ground. A scenario can also pin them by hand:
  "ai_personality_overrides": {"German Reich": {"aggression": 0.9}}
Traits are aggression, ambition, caution, loyalty, trust, all 0.0 to 1.0.
Without either they are procedural, which means reproducible but historically
arbitrary -- this seed gives the 1939 German Reich 0.51 aggression and the
USSR 0.04, so the historical scenarios are worth authoring.


RULES WORTH KNOWING
-------------------

A puppet chooses neither its wars nor its friends. It may only declare war on
its master, may only make peace with its master, and may not join, be invited
into, or leave a faction at all -- it follows its master in and out through
pull_puppets_into_faction. The backstops are queries.can_negotiate_peace and
queries.can_choose_own_faction, enforced where every path converges:
treaty_effects.apply_treaty_effect, plus faction_actions for the join and
leave halves, which crossed invitations reach directly.

A nation that loses its last province leaves every faction, war and pending
offer behind. Only a puppet the player created, or a rebellion put down while
still at war, is additionally erased along with its cores -- which is what
makes an integrated puppet the scenario shipped with keep its cores when
annexed, while one you created and re-annexed does not.

Bombardment is free, draws no return fire, ignores the combat-width cap, and
a bombarding unit still fights on its own tile. The AI shells whenever it has
a target in range, which is also what stops artillery charging into melee.

Multi-turn orders (DISBAND, CONVERT, REPAIR) survive the AI's per-turn
movement re-plan. They did not used to, which meant no AI unit was ever
disbanded by anything.

Combat width is read everywhere, including when moving. Only the top
MAX_COMBAT_ATTACKERS units on a tile fire, so a tile already holding a full
firing line sorts behind every tile with a slot left however much the weights
want it (ai_movement._tile_pressure), and a battle's pull fades as the line
fills -- full strength on an empty tile, nothing at all once it is full. Change
the constant and both halves follow: at 5 a tile stops competing once five are
there, at 10 it keeps asking until ten are. Movement used to reference the
constant nowhere and balanced purely by "fewest units assigned", so stacks came
out at total_units / targets regardless. Measured over 20 seeds at width 5,
that change moved 4% of the army from bodies that could not fire to bodies that
could. The headcount is deliberately a separate dict from the weighted one: the
weights carry a battle pull and a rear-guard push, and a tile at -18 is not a
tile holding minus eighteen men.

Disbanding is a last resort and never touches the front. A unit standing in a
battle or with an enemy next door is never given up, and neither is a type the
nation is currently producing; if every candidate is at the front, nothing is
disbanded at all, because losing the war costs more than the deficit does. The
deficit it answers to is the standing army's, not the standing army's plus
whatever is on order -- counting the queue there had nations deleting real
units to fund units that had not arrived.

Fuel is checked when buying, not only when regretting. Most units burn none,
so a fuel shortage does not stop the purchase loop the way materials do; it
removes fuel-burning units from what is affordable and from what is worth
saving up for, and leaves everything else alone.


WHAT THE AI SAYS WITH THE MODEL OFF
-----------------------------------

data/json/ai_responses.json, read through the same cache as unit_data.json.
A key holds either a plain string or a list of variant groups; a group may
carry a "when" and holds interchangeable "lines". The most specific eligible
group wins and one of its lines is chosen at random, so a nation declaring war
on someone twice its size does not use the sentence it would use on someone
half its size, and neither reads identically twice.

  "when" keys: strength (weaker/even/stronger), at_war, same_faction,
               opinion (hostile/cold/warm)

ai_prompts.resolve() is the accessor; AI_FALLBACK_RESPONSES still exists as a
read-through mapping for the forty-odd sites that have nobody to compare
against. Add lines by editing the JSON -- nothing is compiled in.

A war declaration keeps its words on the queued entry's `prose`, not its
`message`: WAR_DECLARATION is the only proposal with a queued_message and that
is the wargoal the rules act on, so it owns `message`. Until the words were
given their own field they were computed and dropped, and the send site wrote
its own hardcoded sentence -- so every war in the game was declared in
identical words at every immersion level, and PROACTIVE_DECLARE_WAR's nine
lines had never been used. Anything else that ever needs a queued_message will
need the same treatment.

Every prompt states the voice (first person plural, never your own nation in
the third person) via ai_prompts.VOICE_RULE, and a reply that opens by naming
its own sender is dropped for the canned line rather than sent. Small models
need telling; llama3 wrote "Switzerland accepts your offer" as Switzerland.

Which lines the model wrote is recorded. A model's line and the table's used to
be indistinguishable the moment they left ai_evaluation -- the fallback is
substituted by dict.get's default and travels the identical path into the
identical send. `llm` is set at the two places text is authored (_reply, and
the director's per-candidate messages) and rides beside the `message` it
belongs to through pending_diplomacy into both inbox copies, where the
spectator's overview tints those rows orange. Messages written before this
carry no flag and show as ordinary; there is nothing to recover it from.

A message also records the action that produced it, because that lived only on
the sender's pending_diplomacy entry and was deleted on resolution -- so every
treaty announcement read "DIPLOMACY" forever afterwards. constants.
MESSAGE_CATEGORIES turns it into WAR / PEACE / TRADE / FACTION / ACCESS /
PUPPET / ALLIANCE for display only: the `type` field stays TEXT or DIPLOMACY,
because that literal is what raises the popup and draws the gold bubble.


WHAT A SPECTATOR CAN DO
-----------------------

Three switches in constants.py, all default True, all preserving what a
spectator could already do: SPECTATOR_CAN_EDIT_PRODUCTION,
SPECTATOR_CAN_EDIT_RESEARCH, SPECTATOR_CAN_EDIT_APPEARANCE. Turn one off to
hand out the view without the power.

R&D means three different things depending on who clicks it. The map editor
authors what a nation *starts* with (the bulk checkbox list). A spectator picks
a nation and gets its real tech tree, live queue included. A player gets their
own. The chosen nation arrives as map_screen.viewing_research_country, which is
the same hand-off channel editing_country uses for the identity editor.

EVERY REQUEST IN A TURN SENDS THE SAME PROMPT PREFIX. A local model re-reads a
prompt from the first token that differs from the last one it saw, and the
world picture is ~90% of what gets sent. So build_world_context puts everything
true regardless of who is asking first -- date, the nation list, the politics
block, world events -- and only then says who it is talking to, what that
nation owns, and how it regards the others. ai_director's system half names
nobody for the same reason.

And call_ollama sends the user half BEFORE the system half, which is the same
trick one level up. Every caller builds its user half as "<the world> <this
nation's situation> <the task>"; the system half is what differs between the
three phases of a turn. With it in front, a summit prompt, a director prompt
and a proposal reply shared their first eight characters and each phase paid
to read the whole world again -- ~9s of a ~15s share, three times a turn.
Measured on the 1941 map: 0% shared across phases against 84% this way round.
Ollama is the only provider that concatenates the halves; the rest have a
system field of their own.

Measured on the 1941 map, 12 majors, llama3 via Ollama: 179s before, 28s after
-- 15s a call became 2.3s, because 13s of every call was re-reading a world
that had not changed. That is the difference between two of twelve nations
getting a model and all twelve. tests/test_prompt_sharing.py pins it, since
nothing about it is visible from reading a prompt. Neutral relation scores are
left out of the tail for the same budget (63% of them on that map), with the
prompt saying an unlisted nation is neutral.

WHAT A TURN'S MODEL TIME IS ALLOWED TO BE SPENT ON. ai_turn_budget_seconds is
opened once, by prepare_turn, and the three phases take shares of what is left
when each of them starts. Before, each phase read the setting from its own
start: 45 bought 135, and summits -- which run first -- could take a third of
the model's time before the nations a player reads had been asked anything.

Three things a phase must not do with its share:

  * Spend it on work that cannot finish. A summit is five sequential requests
    inside one batch job, and a batch can only give up between jobs, so a
    summit that ran long spent the whole share and was then discarded entire.
    It now checks the clock between its own lines and keeps what was said --
    _complete_exchanges drops an odd trailing line, because one leader speaking
    with nobody answering is not a meeting. And run_llm_batch's min_calls
    declines a share too small to finish the smallest useful piece of work
    (2 for a summit), leaving the time to the next phase instead.

  * Leave the request running when it stops waiting. future.cancel() only
    refuses to start a job and shutdown(wait=False) returns while the worker
    is still blocked reading, so each phase's abandoned request went on
    streaming into the next phase's budget -- 17-19s of it -- and its answer
    was thrown away on arrival. ai_handler.abort_active_requests severs the
    socket, deliberately without abort_ai_generation's model unload: flushing
    VRAM would cost every following call the shared prefix above.

  * Starve work the model never sees. The response phase gathers every
    proposal on the map (192 on the 1941 scenario) but at MAJOR only ~15 of
    them ask the model; the rest are decided from the rules in microseconds.
    They are run outside the batch entirely, so a slow model cannot leave a
    hundred and eighty proposals holding a fallback verdict. The player's own
    correspondence sorts to the front of what is left.

How long a request costs is measured, not assumed -- ai_handler.typical_call_
seconds, smoothed, ignoring requests we severed ourselves. It ranges from ~5s
warm and cache-shared to ~18s on a cold model on the same machine, so nothing
static could stand in for it.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY BUYS. 1941 map, 12 majors, llama3 on one thread. A director
call is ~8s for the first of a turn -- the world moved since the last one, so
the shared prefix does not match and it is re-read -- then ~5s each while it
holds. So the director alone wants ~50s for ten nations, which is why the
default budget is 120 and not 45. Measured over three consecutive turns at 120:
5-6 nations directed per turn, and eleven different nations inside two turns,
because llm_last_turn puts whoever waited longest at the front. At 45 it is 2-3
a turn. The setting has no UI: it lives in data/json/settings_config.json.

When the budget cannot reach everyone, whoever has waited longest goes first.
The batch runs in list order and that order used to be the scenario file's, so
the same nation won the budget every single turn and the rest were never asked
once -- at MAJOR on one thread against a local model, twelve major powers came
down to whichever appeared earliest in the file. nation_data["llm_last_turn"]
is the stamp, written whether or not the reply was usable so a nation with a
failing provider cannot hold the front of the queue. And a batch that runs out
now says so, naming the setting that decides how many it can reach; before,
serving one nation of twelve looked exactly like having nothing to say.
ai_threads is named only when the provider is not Ollama -- one model on one
GPU gets slower when asked to generate several at once, and telling someone to
raise it there would be advice that makes their next turn worse. A phase that
declined its share (min_calls above) says so on the console and not to the
player: nothing was spent, so there is nothing to account for.

At ABSOLUTE, a nation with only one legal option is still asked -- choosing
and phrasing come from the same call, so skipping it silently skipped the
wording. Measured on the 1939 map, that took the director from 19.6 to 22.6
calls a turn: the cost lever is still doing its job, since most nations still
have nothing on their menu at all. If a turn feels slow at ABSOLUTE the thing
to check is ai_threads (1 by default) and whether the provider is actually
answering -- an unreachable Ollama makes every call wait out its timeout, and
ai_turn_budget_seconds then caps each turn at that budget rather than the
work.
